Savart Wheel

Spin a toothed wheel against a flexible card. At low speeds, individual impacts sound like separate clicks. As the wheel speeds up, the clicks join together and create a musical pitch.

Start the wheel, then lower the card until it touches the teeth. Change the speed and number of teeth to investigate pitch.

What does the wheel demonstrate?

Each tooth produces one impact when it strikes the card. The impacts occur slowly when the wheel turns slowly, so they can be heard as separate clicks. As the impacts become faster and more regular, the ear begins to perceive them as a continuous musical tone.

Doubling the wheel speed doubles the frequency. Keeping the same speed while doubling the number of teeth also doubles the frequency, producing a pitch approximately one octave higher.

Experiment: Set the wheel to 24 teeth and note its frequency. Then change it to 48 teeth without changing the RPM. What happens to the frequency and musical note?
